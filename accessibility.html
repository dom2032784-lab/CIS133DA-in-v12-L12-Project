Our Commitment to Accessibility

Our website is designed with accessibility in mind to ensure all users can interact with our content in a meaningful way. We aim to reduce barriers by providing clear navigation, readable content, and inclusive design features that support individuals with a wide range of abilities and assistive technologies.

Web Users and Web Accessibility

Overview of disabilities, barriers, and accessibility principles
Disability Description Common Barriers Accessibility Principles to Apply
Auditory Users who are deaf or hard of hearing may rely on visual content instead of audio. Audio-only content, lack of captions, and missing transcripts limit access. Perceivable: Provide captions, transcripts, and visual alternatives for audio content.
Cognitive and Neurological Includes users with learning disabilities, memory limitations, or attention-related challenges. Complex navigation, unclear instructions, and overwhelming layouts can make content difficult to use. Understandable: Use clear language, consistent layouts, and simple instructions.
Physical Users with limited mobility may rely on keyboards or assistive devices instead of a mouse. Small clickable areas and lack of keyboard navigation create barriers. Operable: Ensure all features are accessible via keyboard and provide large clickable elements.
Speech Users with speech disabilities may have difficulty using voice-based systems. Websites that rely only on voice input or lack alternative input options limit usability. Operable: Provide multiple input methods beyond voice interaction.
Visual Includes users who are blind, have low vision, or color blindness. Poor contrast, missing alt text, and unreadable fonts make content inaccessible. Perceivable: Use high contrast, readable fonts, and descriptive alt text for images.
